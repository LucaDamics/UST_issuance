The premium mirror: SSA vs HHS monthly wedges

One retiree makes it concrete. Social Security owes them $2,000 and their $185 Medicare Part B premium is deducted before the check goes out. Cash (DTS): $1,815 leaves the Treasury account, all labelled Social Security; no premium cash ever moves on its own. Budget accounting (MTS): a $2,000 Social Security outlay, and Medicare outlays reduced by the $185 premium. Sum that over every beneficiary (~$15bn a month) and you get the two lines below: DTS-built SSA runs below MTS by the withheld premiums (blue), and DTS-built Medicare/HHS runs above MTS by the same premiums (orange). Two wedges, one mechanism, cancelling at the total — which is how you know the mapping is capturing real structure, not noise.

The payoff: the seasonal model, Oct 2022 through Dec 2027

Per-bucket shares from ten years of MTS Table 9 history (median over FY2016–19 + FY2023–25, COVID excluded) plus the payment-calendar rule: a month-start benefit block (Medicare capitation, VA, SSI, military pay; $64bn in FY2023, extrapolated to ~$90bn+ ahead) moves into the prior month when the 1st lands on a weekend or Labor Day. Left of the divider: in-sample reconstruction of FY2023–25 from each year’s actual totals (mean |error| $53bn, dominated by credit-reform months). Right: CBO’s baseline split into months — FY2026 scored $30bn mean absolute error over the nine published months, all signs right, zero bias — then the true forecast through FY2027 and calendar Q4 2027 (FY2028 baseline). Honest out-of-sample yardstick: leave-one-year-out cross-validation (shares from the other years only, extrapolated calendar block) scores $17–40bn/month in ordinary years and ~$96bn in FY2023 (debt ceiling + the student-loan reversal) — about 30% of the mean monthly deficit overall, so read FY2026’s 15% as the friendly end of that range. The forecast months additionally carry CBO’s own level risk (year-1 deficit RMSE ≈ 0.7% of GDP) and customs/tariff policy risk.

Inside the month: the cumulative daily deficit path

Mean cumulative deficit by business day of the month, from the daily DTS (Mar 2023–Jun 2026), budget-classified through the same mapping as everything above. Shaded bands are the interquartile range across months — the honest uncertainty display, because below monthly frequency there is no external benchmark: the MTS is monthly and the DTS is itself the primary source. Two regimes, split where they genuinely differ: in tax months (Jan/Apr/Jun/Sep/Dec) the mid-month estimated and corporate payments around the 15th pull the month back toward balance (ending −$30bn on average); regular months grind steadily to +$243bn. Both start the same way: the first three business days absorb the benefit block. By construction each path sums to the month totals reconciled against the MTS above.

The weekly rhythm

Mean receipts and outlays per week-of-month (business-day weeks; week 5 is the 1–3 day tail). The shape that matters for bill issuance: week 1 runs a ~$73bn deficit as the month-start benefit block clears, weeks 2–3 roughly balance as withholding and mid-month tax dates build to their week-3 peak, and the deficit reopens in week 4 and the month-end tail (interest and late-month outlays against thin receipts). This is the within-month funding gap the TGA and bill settlement calendar have to bridge.
