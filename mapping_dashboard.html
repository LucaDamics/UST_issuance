The premium mirror: SSA vs HHS monthly wedges

One retiree makes it concrete. Social Security owes them $2,000 and their $185 Medicare Part B premium is deducted before the check goes out. Cash (DTS): $1,815 leaves the Treasury account, all labelled Social Security; no premium cash ever moves on its own. Budget accounting (MTS): a $2,000 Social Security outlay, and Medicare outlays reduced by the $185 premium. Sum that over every beneficiary (~$15bn a month) and you get the two lines below: DTS-built SSA runs below MTS by the withheld premiums (blue), and DTS-built Medicare/HHS runs above MTS by the same premiums (orange). Two wedges, one mechanism, cancelling at the total — which is how you know the mapping is capturing real structure, not noise.

The daily model, validated day by day

Not an average month: a dated model. Each business day is classified by what it is — first three business days (benefit block), the day covering the 15th (tax dates, coupons, military pay), the 2nd/3rd/4th Wednesdays (Social Security cycles), the last two days, with shifted variants when a month starts on a non-business day — and each bucket learns its share of the month landing on each day type. Trained on Mar 2023– Sep 2025, tested walk-forward on the nine FY2026 months of held-out daily actuals (this layer does have a benchmark). Daily deficit MAE $12bn vs $21bn for a uniform spread; within-month cumulative path $34bn vs $52bn; weekly $34bn vs $56bn. The chart shows the fiscal-year-to-date cumulative deficit, day by day — monthly totals are actuals here, so this isolates pure within-month shape skill. One calendar find along the way: the DTS follows Fed banking days (Saturday holidays are not observed on Friday) — verified and encoded.

Portfolio consequences: bill share and WAM, 2015 → 2027

With Treasury holding nominal coupon sizes steady (its stated posture, TBAC provisional recommendations), bills absorb the residual financing need — so the bill share of marketable debt drifts up from 21.1% (Jun 2026 actual, exact per-CUSIP MSPD computation) to ~24–25% by end-2027, further above TBAC’s 15–20% guidance band, while the portfolio’s weighted average maturity shortens from 70.7 to ~69 months. History is quarterly MSPD snapshots; the forecast evolves the same universe with engine redemptions, TBAC-size coupon issuance on each tenor, and the solved net-bill path (Q3 2026 bridged with Treasury’s own $739bn borrowing plan). The steady-coupon assumption is the dial: any coupon-size increase in 2027 refundings would bend both lines.
